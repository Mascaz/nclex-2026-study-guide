## Question 1 (Multiple Response (SATA))
Case Study 1: Oncology (Neutropenic Fever to Sepsis)

Clinical Scenario: 0800

The nurse is caring for a 45-year-old female admitted to the oncology unit. The client received her second cycle of chemotherapy for breast cancer 10 days ago. She presents with chills, fatigue, and a cough.

Admission Note: Client reports feeling profoundly tired for the last 24 hours. Vital signs: BP 105/65, HR 112 bpm, RR 22, Temp 102.1°F (38.9°C), SpO2 95% on room air. Mucous membranes are dry. Client has a productive cough with yellow sputum. An implanted port is present in the right upper chest without erythema. Which findings are the priority for the nurse to follow up on?

Select all that apply.

- A. BP 105/65
- B. HR 112 bpm
- C. RR 22
- D. Temp 102.1°F (38.9°C)
- E. SpO2 95%
- F. Dry mucous membranes
- G. Productive cough with yellow sputum
- H. Received chemotherapy 10 days ago

**Correct Answer:** B. HR 112 bpm | D. Temp 102.1°F (38.9°C) | G. Productive cough with yellow sputum | H. Received chemotherapy 10 days ago

**Rationale:**
A temperature >100.4°F in a client 7-14 days post-chemotherapy is a medical emergency (neutropenic fever). Tachycardia and a productive cough indicate a potential systemic infection.

• Options A, C, E, & F: BP 105/65, RR 22, and SpO2 95% are within or near normal limits at this time. Dry mucous membranes are a sign of mild dehydration but are not the immediate life-threatening priority. A port without erythema is a normal finding.

## Question 3 (Multiple Response (SATA))
Clinical Update: 1000

The client’s CBC returns. WBC is 0.9 x 10^3/uL (Normal: 4.5-11.0), and Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) is 400/mm3 (Normal: 1500-8000). Blood pressure drops to 88/50 mmHg. Based on the updated clinical data, which of the following are contraindicated for this client?

Select all that apply.

- A. Initiate neutropenic precautions.
- B. Administer a 500 mL normal saline IV bolus.
- C. Allow the client to have a fresh fruit salad for breakfast.
- D. Insert an indwelling urinary catheter to monitor output.
- E. Administer a prescribed rectal suppository for constipation.
- F. Take an oral or axillary temperature.
- G. Place fresh flowers sent by the family at the bedside.

**Correct Answer:** C. Allow the client to have a fresh fruit salad for breakfast. | D. Insert an indwelling urinary catheter to monitor output. | E. Administer a prescribed rectal suppository for constipation. | G. Place fresh flowers sent by the family at the bedside.

**Rationale:**
Correct Answers: C, D, E, & G

For a profoundly neutropenic patient, certain everyday items and routine procedures pose immediate, life-threatening risks and must be strictly avoided. Fresh fruits, vegetables, and live plants or flowers are contraindicated because they frequently harbor dangerous environmental bacteria and fungi, such as Aspergillus or Pseudomonas. Furthermore, the nurse must avoid any invasive procedures that break intact skin or mucosal barriers, as these defenses are the patient’s last line of protection. Interventions such as inserting a Foley catheter or performing any rectal care, including suppositories, enemas, or rectal temperatures, are strictly prohibited. Even microscopic mucosal tears from these procedures provide a direct pathway for normal gut flora and opportunistic pathogens to enter the bloodstream, rapidly leading to systemic bacteremia.

• Options A, B, & F: An ANC less than 500/mm3 indicates severe neutropenia, making the immediate initiation of protective/neutropenic precautions vital to minimize exposure to pathogens. The drop in blood pressure (BP 88/50 mmHg) indicates inadequate tissue perfusion and progression toward septic shock, making aggressive crystalloid fluid resuscitation (Normal Saline bolus) a medical priority. Monitoring temperature is critical to track infection trends; however, it must be done via a non-invasive route (oral or axillary).

## Question 4 (Multiple Response (SATA))
The client’s blood pressure drops to 88/50 mmHg, and the provider diagnoses the client with septic shock. Which of the following orders should the nurse anticipate implementing? 

Select all that apply.

- A. Administer a rapid IV fluid bolus
- B. Apply cooling blankets to reduce the core body temperature
- C. Monitor Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) continuously
- D. Prepare the client for emergent hemodialysis
- E. Administer IV vasopressors as prescribed
- F. Draw blood to assess lactic acid levels
- G. Monitor serum uric acid levels

**Correct Answer:** A. Administer a rapid IV fluid bolus | C. Monitor Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) continuously | E. Administer IV vasopressors as prescribed | F. Draw blood to assess lactic acid levels

**Rationale:**
The client’s active infection and dangerous drop in blood pressure indicate septic shock, which requires immediate interventions to restore tissue perfusion. The priority action is to administer aggressive IV fluids (A) to boost blood volume. If fluids fail to raise the blood pressure, IV vasopressors (E) are required to constrict blood vessels and increase pressure. To verify that blood and oxygen are effectively reaching the vital organs, the nurse must closely monitor the Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) (C) and lactic acid levels (F), as elevated lactic acid indicates cellular starvation and poor perfusion.

• Option B: Apply cooling blankets: This is contraindicated because it can induce shivering, which significantly increases the body’s metabolic demand and oxygen consumption, worsening the shock state.

• Options D & G: Hemodialysis and Uric acid levels: These are interventions and monitoring parameters for Tumor Lysis Syndrome (a condition caused by massive cancer cell breakdown), not for septic shock.

## Question 5 (Single Choice)
At 1400, the nurse reassesses the client with neutropenic septic shock following the administration of aggressive IV fluid resuscitation and broad-spectrum antibiotics. Which of the following findings provides the most definitive clinical evidence that these interventions have been successful in restoring systemic tissue perfusion?

- A. The serum lactic acid level increases from 2.5 to 4.0 mmol/L
- B. Total urine output drops to 15 mL/hr over the last two hours
- C. Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) stabilizes at 72 mmHg
- D. The Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) shifts to 350/mm3

**Correct Answer:** C. Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) stabilizes at 72 mmHg

**Rationale:**
Correct Answer: C. Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) stabilizes at 72 mmHg

In the management of septic shock, the ultimate clinical goal is to restore macrovascular and microvascular blood flow to vital organs. A Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) greater than 65 mmHg is the gold standard benchmark indicating that fluid resuscitation and vasopressor therapy have successfully generated enough systemic pressure to adequately perfuse peripheral tissues and target organs. A MAP of 72 mmHg proves that the collaborative interventions are actively reversing the distributive shock state.

• Option A: The serum lactic acid level increases from 2.5 to 4.0 mmol/L: An increasing lactic acid level is a highly concerning sign of worsening anaerobic metabolism. It indicates that tissues are still starved of oxygen, meaning the current shock interventions are failing, not succeeding. A decreasing trend is what the nurse wants to see.

• Option B: Total urine output drops to 15 mL/hr over the last two hours: Safe and adequate renal perfusion is traditionally demonstrated by a urine output of at least 30 mL/hr (or 0.5 mL/kg/hr). A drop to 15 mL/hr indicates persistent acute kidney injury (AKI) resulting from poor renal blood flow and inadequate resuscitation.

• Option D: The Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) shifts to 350/mm3: While the client’s severe neutropenia is the root cause of her vulnerability to infection, an ANC of 350/mm3 remains critically low and leaves her deeply immunocompromised. Furthermore, bone marrow recovery takes time and will not show immediate improvement during the acute phase of fluid resuscitation for septic shock.

## Question 6 (Multiple Response (SATA))
Case Study 2: Maternity (Preeclampsia to HELLP)

Clinical Scenario: 1400

Triage Assessment: Client reports feeling “puffy” in her face and hands over the last three days. She states her baby is moving normally. She reports blurry vision and “seeing spots” since this morning. Vital signs: BP 168/110 mmHg, HR 88, RR 18. Fetal heart rate is 145 bpm with moderate variability. Client notes mild epigastric discomfort after eating lunch.

The nurse reviews the client’s triage assessment. Which of the following findings are most concerning for a severe hypertensive disorder of pregnancy? 

Select all that apply.

- A. Client reports feeling "puffy" in her face and hands.
- B. Fetal heart rate is 145 bpm with moderate variability.
- C. Client reports blurry vision and "seeing spots."
- D. Blood pressure is 168/110 mmHg.
- E. Client notes mild epigastric discomfort.

**Correct Answer:** C. Client reports blurry vision and "seeing spots." | D. Blood pressure is 168/110 mmHg. | E. Client notes mild epigastric discomfort.

**Rationale:**
Visual disturbances (blurry vision/spots), severe hypertension (systolic > 160 or diastolic > 110), and epigastric or right upper quadrant (RUQ) pain indicating liver involvement are severe, hallmark features of preeclampsia.

• Option A: “Puffy” face and hands (edema) is a common finding in normal pregnancies and is no longer a diagnostic criterion for preeclampsia.

• Option B: A fetal heart rate of 145 bpm with moderate variability is a reassuring, normal finding.

## Question 7 (Single Choice)
Clinical Update: 1430

Stat labs are drawn. Results: Platelets 85,000/mm3, AST 112 U/L, ALT 125 U/L, Hemoglobin 9.2 g/dL. The nurse correlates the client’s clinical findings to the underlying pathophysiology of preeclampsia. The nurse understands that the client’s epigastric pain and elevated liver enzymes (AST 112 U/L, ALT 125 U/L) are primarily caused by which of the following pathophysiological processes?

- A. Bone marrow suppression
- B. Renal vasospasm
- C. Liver ischemia
- D. Decreased cardiac output

**Correct Answer:** C. Liver ischemia

**Rationale:**
Correct Answer: C. Liver ischemia

Preeclampsia is a disease of generalized vasospasm and endothelial damage. This systemic vasospasm causes liver ischemia, leading to elevated liver enzymes (AST/ALT) and the classic symptom of epigastric or right upper quadrant pain.

• Option A: Bone marrow suppression is not the cause of low platelets in pregnancy; rather, platelets aggregate at sites of endothelial damage.

• Option B: Renal vasospasm causes proteinuria, not epigastric pain. 

• Option D: Decreased cardiac output does not directly cause localized epigastric pain in this context.

- A. Keep calcium gluconate readily available at the bedside.
- B. Restrict total oral and IV fluid intake to 125 mL/hr.
- C. Ambulate the client to the bathroom every 2 hours to prevent DVT.
- D. Assess deep tendon reflexes (DTRs) every 1-2 hours.

**Rationale:**
Correct Answers: A, B, D, & E

## Question 11 (Multiple Response (SATA))
Case Study 3: Med-Surg (Pneumonia to ARDS)

Clinical Scenario: 1000

A 68-year-old male is admitted with left lower lobe community-acquired pneumonia. He is receiving oxygen at 4 L/min via nasal cannula. The nurse completes a respiratory assessment. Which of the following findings indicate that the client’s respiratory status is deteriorating?

Assessment: Client is restless and attempting to pull off his oxygen tubing. He is using accessory muscles to breathe. Lung sounds reveal crackles in the left lower lobe. SpO2 is 86% despite 4 L/min oxygen. Capillary refill is 2 seconds. Skin is cool and clammy.

Select all that apply.

- A. Client is restless and attempting to pull off his oxygen tubing
- B. Client is using accessory muscles to breathe.
- C. Lung sounds reveal crackles in the left lower lobe.
- D. SpO2 is 86% despite 4 L/min oxygen.
- E. Capillary refill is 2 seconds.

**Correct Answer:** A. Client is restless and attempting to pull off his oxygen tubing | B. Client is using accessory muscles to breathe. | D. SpO2 is 86% despite 4 L/min oxygen.

**Rationale:**
Restlessness, agitation, or confusion are often the earliest signs of hypoxia. Accessory muscle use indicates a significantly increased work of breathing and impending respiratory fatigue. Refractory hypoxemia (a low SpO2 that does not improve despite supplemental oxygen) is a hallmark sign of severe respiratory failure and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS).

• Option C: Crackles in the left lower lobe are an expected, baseline finding for a client already diagnosed with left lower lobe pneumonia.

• Option E: A capillary refill time of 2 seconds is a normal finding.

## Question 16 (Multiple Response (SATA))
Case Study 4: Endocrine (Diabetic Ketoacidosis)

Clinical Scenario: 1800

A 19-year-old male with a history of Type 1 Diabetes is brought to the ED by his roommate. The roommate states, “He has been vomiting for two days and sleeping constantly.”

The nurse performs an initial assessment. Which of the following findings strongly suggest the client is experiencing Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)? 

Select all that apply.

- A. Client is lethargic and oriented to person only.
- B. The client's breath has a fruity, sweet odor
- C. Respirations are deep and rapid at 32 breaths per minute.
- D. Skin is flushed and extremely dry
- E. Capillary blood glucose reads "HIGH" on the monitor.
- F. Client has 2+ pitting edema in the lower extremities.

**Correct Answer:** B. The client's breath has a fruity, sweet odor | C. Respirations are deep and rapid at 32 breaths per minute. | D. Skin is flushed and extremely dry | E. Capillary blood glucose reads "HIGH" on the monitor.

**Rationale:**
Correct Answers: B, C, D, & E

A fruity breath odor indicates the body is blowing-off ketones. Deep, rapid breathing (Kussmaul respirations) is a compensatory mechanism to blow off CO2 due to severe metabolic acidosis. Flushed, dry skin and a “HIGH” glucose reading indicate profound dehydration and hyperglycemia, which are the classic hallmarks of DKA.

• Option A: Lethargy is concerning but non-specific and could indicate many conditions (like head trauma or overdose). 

• Option F: Edema is related to fluid overload, whereas DKA causes severe fluid deficit (dehydration).

## Question 17 (Single Choice)
Clinical Update: 1815

Stat labs: Blood Glucose 650 mg/dL, pH 7.15, HCO3 12 mEq/L, Potassium 5.2 mEq/L, Urine ketones: Large. 

The nurse reviews the client’s laboratory results. Which of the following findings is unexpected for a client experiencing DKA and requires immediate clarification?

- A. pH 7.15
- B. HCO3 12 mEq/L
- C. Potassium 5.2 mEq/L
- D. Urine specific gravity 1.005

**Correct Answer:** D. Urine specific gravity 1.005

**Rationale:**
Correct Answer: D. Urine specific gravity 1.005

A urine specific gravity of 1.005 indicates highly dilute, watery urine. Because DKA causes osmotic diuresis and severe systemic dehydration, the expected finding would be a highly elevated (concentrated) urine specific gravity.

• Option A & B: DKA causes metabolic acidosis, making a low pH (Option A) and low HCO3 (Option B) expected findings. 

• Option C: The initial serum potassium is often normal or high (5.2) because severe acidosis drives potassium out of the cells and into the bloodstream, even though total body potassium is actually depleted.

## Question 18 (Single Choice)
After reviewing the client’s critical laboratory results (Blood Glucose 650 mg/dL, pH 7.15, Potassium 5.2 mEq/L), the provider enters a set of stat orders for the management of Diabetic Ketoacidosis.

Which of the following orders must the nurse implement first?

- A. Administer a 10-unit regular insulin IV bolus
- B. Start a continuous IV insulin infusion at 0.1 units/kg/hr
- C. Infuse 1000 mL of 0.9% Normal Saline over 1 hour
- D. Administer Sodium Bicarbonate IV push

**Correct Answer:** C. Infuse 1000 mL of 0.9% Normal Saline over 1 hour

**Rationale:**
Correct Answer: C. Infuse 1000 mL of 0.9% Normal Saline over 1 hour

The absolute first priority in DKA management is aggressive fluid resuscitation. Severe dehydration can quickly lead to hypovolemic shock. Isotonic fluids must be initiated before insulin, because insulin will rapidly shift water and potassium back into the cells, worsening intravascular volume depletion and causing severe hypotension if the vascular space isn’t filled first.

• Options A & B: Insulin is the crucial second step, initiated only after fluid resuscitation has begun. 

• Option D: Bicarbonate is rarely used in DKA unless the pH drops below 6.9, as it can cause paradoxical intracellular acidosis.

## Question 19 (Multiple Response (SATA))
The client is diagnosed with Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA). Which of the following interventions and monitoring parameters should the nurse include in the active plan of care?

- A. Initiate a continuous Regular insulin IV drip.
- B. Administer NPH insulin intravenously.
- C. Monitor continuous cardiac rhythm.
- D. Restrict oral and IV fluid intake.
- E. Monitor serum potassium levels and the anion gap.

**Correct Answer:** A. Initiate a continuous Regular insulin IV drip. | C. Monitor continuous cardiac rhythm. | E. Monitor serum potassium levels and the anion gap.

**Rationale:**
Correct Answers: A, C, & E

Treatment for DKA involves a continuous Regular insulin IV drip (A): Regular insulin is the only type of insulin that can be given intravenously. The anion gap and potassium levels (E) are monitored frequently to ensure the acidosis is resolving.

• Option B: NPH is an intermediate-acting insulin and is never given IV. 

• Option D: Fluid restriction is strictly contraindicated; these clients require massive fluid resuscitation.

## Question 20 (Single Choice)
Clinical Update: 2200

The client has received 3 liters of normal saline. The regular insulin drip is infusing at 5 units/hr. The current blood glucose is 240 mg/dL.

Based on the client’s most recent blood glucose of 240 mg/dL, what is the most appropriate nursing action?

- A. Stop the continuous IV insulin infusion immediately.
- B. Administer 1 ampule of 50% Dextrose (D50) IV push.
- C. Change the IV fluids to 5% Dextrose in 0.45% Normal Saline (D5 1/2 NS).
- D. Increase the rate of the regular insulin infusion to 8 units/hr.

**Correct Answer:** C. Change the IV fluids to 5% Dextrose in 0.45% Normal Saline (D5 1/2 NS).

**Rationale:**
Correct Answer: C. Change the IV fluids to 5% Dextrose in 0.45% Normal Saline (D5 1/2 NS).

When blood glucose in a DKA patient drops to around 200-250 mg/dL, dextrose must be added to the maintenance IV fluids. The insulin drip must continue running to clear the blood of ketones and completely close the anion gap. Adding dextrose to the IV fluids prevents the client from crashing into life-threatening hypoglycemia while the insulin finishes fixing the acidosis.

• Option A: Stopping the insulin will cause ketone production to restart, halting DKA resolution.

• Option B: D50 IV push is reserved for severe, symptomatic hypoglycemia, not a BG of 240.

• Option D: Increasing insulin without adding glucose will cause rapid, dangerous hypoglycemia.
